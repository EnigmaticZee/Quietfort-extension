What it does

Everything you'd expect. A few things you wouldn't.

Quietfort uses the same trusted filter lists as the best blockers, then adds tools for the bits of the web that lists can't fix.

Ads, gone

Banners, pop-ups, pop-unders and video ads, including the ones that try to sneak back in after the page loads.

Trackers stopped

Analytics beacons, fingerprinting scripts and tracking pixels are blocked before they load. Global Privacy Control is on by default.

Cookie banners, rejected

Quietfort answers consent pop-ups for you, always choosing "reject" or "necessary only", never "accept".

Dangerous sites, flagged

Known phishing, scam and malware sites get a clear warning page first, so one wrong click doesn't cost you.

Zap and hide

Click anything to remove it for this visit, or hide it on that site for good. Or just describe it: "the newsletter pop-up".

Site broken? One button.

If a page stops working, Quietfort finds the exact rule that broke it and switches only that one off, not your whole protection.

Zap mode

See something you don't want? Click it away.

Turn on Zap from the panel or the right-click menu, then click the things you'd rather not see. They vanish at once and come back when you reload.

  • Undo any removal with one click or ⌘Z.
  • Nothing is saved. For a permanent fix, use Hide an element.
  • Links don't fire while zapping, so you never open a page by accident.

Privacy

Your browsing stays yours.

A blocker sees every page you open, so it has to be trustworthy. Quietfort runs entirely in your browser, and its code is public so anyone can check.

  1. Unzip quietfort-chrome.zip.

Questions

Good to know

Is Quietfort really free?

Yes. No trial, no subscription, and no ads let through for money. The code is open source under the MPL-2.0.

Will it slow my browser down?

No. Blocking happens inside the browser's own fast filtering engine, and because ads and trackers never load, most pages finish sooner.

A site broke. What do I do?

Open the panel and press Site broken?. Quietfort finds the rule causing the problem and turns off only that one. You can also switch blocking off for the site with one toggle.

Where do the filter lists come from?

From their volunteer maintainers: EasyList, EasyPrivacy, the uBlock Origin and AdGuard lists, and others. Your browser downloads them directly; they're credited in the extension's notices.

What do the AI features do?

They're optional and off by default. Where your browser has an on-device model, it helps find ads and pop-ups you describe in plain words. It runs on your device; nothing is sent anywhere.

How do I report a problem?

Open an issue on GitHub. For security problems, please use the private report form described in SECURITY.md.
